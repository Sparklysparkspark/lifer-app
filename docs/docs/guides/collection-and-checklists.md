# Collection and checklists

The Collection page is Lifer's home screen. It shows the species checklist for a region, with everything you've photographed in full color.

![The Collection page](/img/collection.jpg)

## What the checklists cover {#groups}

Each country's checklist is split into groups, and you download only the groups you want from [Regions](./offline-packs-and-map.md):

| Group                     | What's in it                                                                                                                            |
| ------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Birds**                 | Every bird species                                                                                                                      |
| **Mammals**               | Land mammals                                                                                                                            |
| **Fish**                  | Bony fish, plus nearby [ocean areas](#sea-zones) for coastal regions                                                                    |
| **Reptiles & Amphibians** | **Reptiles**, **Turtles** and **Amphibians**, each its own group                                                                        |
| **Marine Invertebrates**  | **Corals**, **Jellies & Anemones**, **Echinoderms**, **Nudibranchs**, **Shells & Marine Mollusks**, **Cephalopods** and **Crustaceans** |

Sharks, rays and marine mammals like whales, dolphins and seals are included in the **Fish** download. Sponges and tunicates aren't covered yet. **Species downloads** only offers the groups that have data for the countries you pick.

Marine invertebrates aren't given a [rarity](#rarity-tiers) yet, since there aren't enough records to rate them fairly. Their species page shows IUCN status instead, where it's known.

For anything else, like insects, spiders, plants or fungi, add species from iNaturalist. See [Insects, plants and other groups](#other-taxa).

### Add more groups from the Collection {#add-groups}

Click **+ Add species** at the top of the Collection, then **Download animal groups**. It lists every group for the countries you're looking at:

- On a country or one of its provinces, that country.
- On a continent, World or **All species**, the countries you've downloaded there. To add a country, click **Download more countries** in the same menu, which opens [Species downloads](../settings.md#regions).

Groups you already have for all of those countries are ticked and greyed out. Tick the ones you want, check the size, then click **Download**. For example, on North America with Canada and the United States downloaded, ticking **Reptiles** downloads reptiles for both. The dialog closes at once and the download runs in the background, with its progress at the bottom left. When every group is already downloaded, the menu says so.

## Choosing a region {#regions}

- With no region chosen, click **Browse by region →** or one of your downloaded countries.
- Once you're in a region, the breadcrumb at the top (**All species / World / continent / country**) takes you back out.
- **Areas in** lists the provinces, states or other areas inside the region, so you can go a level deeper. Downloading a country adds its provinces and states, so you can also find them with search. A country you haven't downloaded has a **Show provinces/states** button.
- World and continent views combine the checklists of the countries you've downloaded, marked "(downloaded countries only)".
- With the [offline map](./offline-packs-and-map.md#offline-map) downloaded, **Show map** / **Hide map** next to the breadcrumb shows the region's outline.

### Species pins on the map {#species-pins}

The region's map shows a pin wherever you photographed a species there, in the colour of its group (birds, mammals and so on). Nearby pins merge into a numbered circle as you zoom out.

A pin goes where the photo was taken. A region's map shows the photos taken inside it or at sea within 22 km of its coast, whatever region they're filed under, plus every photo filed under it, even one taken farther away. World and continent views have no outline, so they show the photos filed under their countries.

- Hover a pin to see your best photo of that species there and how many you took.
- Click a pin, or the photo in its card, to look through that species' photos from there, best first. Use the arrow keys to move between them and Esc to go back to the map. **View species**, in the card or the viewer, opens the species page.
- Click a numbered circle, pinch, or double-click to zoom in; once you've zoomed in, scrolling zooms too, and drag looks around. At the first view, scrolling moves the page. **Reset view** goes back to the whole region.
- Hover over the map (or tab to it) and the layers button appears at the top right, with the same choices as the [photo map](./photo-map.md#online): **Map** or **Satellite**, and **Online detail** set to **Automatic** or **Off**. Zoomed in and online, Automatic loads street and trail detail by itself. A choice made here applies on the photo map too, and the other way round. The (i) button at the bottom right lists the map's sources and licences.
- To turn them off, clear **Show map pins** in the **View** menu. Lifer remembers your choice.
- Only photos with coordinates get a pin. A photo with only a country or province has no exact spot: give it one from the [Needs placing](./photo-map.md#needs-placing) list.

Lifer remembers the last region you viewed. Filters, sorting and grouping are part of the page address, so you can bookmark a view.

If a region isn't downloaded, the page offers a button to **Download** it. Species you've photographed there, or [added to its checklist yourself](#add-to-checklist), still show below the button.

## Reading your progress

The top bar shows **X / Y photographed** for the view you're looking at: the region, and any groups you've filtered to. Inside a region, a progress bar shows **N of M photographed** and **N seen** for that region. When the view includes birds, there's also a link to the region's **eBird Illustrated Checklist**.

When a filter hides some cards, the toolbar says **Showing N of M**.

Species cards look different depending on your status:

| Card            | Meaning                                              |
| --------------- | ---------------------------------------------------- |
| Full color      | **Photographed**: you have your own photo of it      |
| Faded with a ✓  | **Seen**: you've seen it but not photographed it yet |
| Gray            | Not seen yet                                         |
| ◎ in the corner | On your targets                                      |

Archived species and extinct species never count toward totals.

### Counting only photos from here {#count-photos-from}

By default a species counts as photographed on every checklist once you've photographed it anywhere. To keep a life list for one place, open **View** and, under **Counting**, pick from **Include photos from**: **Anywhere**, the country, or (on a state or province) the province itself. On a country you choose between Anywhere and the country.

With a country or province picked, a species counts only if you have a photo of it taken there (in the Trash or hidden doesn't count). Everything else shows as not photographed: the count, the progress bar, the cards, **Photographed first** and the photographed filters all follow. Seen marks and targets don't change. The choice is saved as a level, so picking the province and then opening a country counts that country's photos, and the next province you open counts its own. It isn't offered on a continent or World.

## Seen, targets, hiding and archiving {#card-actions}

Open a species card's **⋯** menu:

- **Mark as seen** / **Mark as unseen**: for species you've seen but not photographed. To mark all your birds at once, [import your eBird data](./ebird-import.md).
- **Add to targets** / **Remove from targets**: species you're going after. This works for photographed species too, if you want a better photo.
- **Archive species**: leaves a species out of your lists and counts everywhere, for species you're not interested in. Nothing is deleted.
- **Hide from this region**: takes a species off this region's checklist only, for example a one-off vagrant. In a province you can tick the province, **All of** the country, or both. Hiding it from a country also hides it from every province in that country. Its photos and its place on other checklists aren't affected.
- **Remove from this checklist**: only for species you added to this checklist yourself. See [Adding a species to a checklist](#add-to-checklist).
- **Remove species**: only for species you added from iNaturalist. See [Other groups](#other-taxa).

To archive a whole family at once, set **Group** to **Family group** and click **Archive group** on the family's heading.

You can't hide or archive species you've already photographed.

After you hide or archive a species, mark it as seen or add it to your targets, a message at the bottom of the screen offers **Undo**.

### Undoing hiding and archiving {#undoing-hide-and-archive}

- **Settings > Library > Hidden species** lists everything you've hidden, by country and province. Choose **Unhide** from a species' **⋯** menu (or right-click it), or click **Unhide all shown** at the top.
- **Settings > Library > Archived species** lists them by family. Choose **Unarchive** from a species' **⋯** menu (or right-click it), or **Unarchive all** from a family heading's **⋯** menu. You can also choose **Unarchive species** from the species page menu.

## Adding a species to a checklist yourself {#add-to-checklist}

A checklist can miss a species you know is there, like an insect you imported by hand for one province that you also find in the next, or a sea turtle off a coast whose [ocean area](#sea-zones) lists only fish. You can add any species Lifer has to any country's, province's or ocean area's checklist.

**From the checklist:** open the region and click **+ Add species**, then **Add one species by hand**. Search by name, then pick the species (or use the arrow keys and <kbd>Enter</kbd>). If you've ticked ocean areas under **Include nearby water:**, choose under **Add it to** whether it goes on the region's checklist or one of the ocean areas'. **Mark as seen** is on to start with, so the species also counts as seen; untick it to only add it to the list. If no species matches, the search says so, and points you to turning on iNaturalist search in Settings when it's off.

**From the species page:** click **Add to another checklist**, then either:

- choose **Country or province**, and pick the country or the province or state, or
- choose **Ocean area**, and search for it by name.

Then click **Add to** it. World and the continents have no checklist of their own, so pick a region inside them.

A species you added:

- Shows on that checklist with an **Added by you** label, and counts toward its totals. One added to a province also shows on its country's checklist.
- Shows even if its group isn't downloaded for the region, or **Hide species almost nobody photographs** would hide it, since you asked for it. On a region that isn't downloaded yet, it shows below the **Download** button.
- On an ocean area, shows on a coastal region's checklist when that ocean area is ticked under **Include nearby water:**, like the ocean area's own fish. An ocean area you've added species to is offered there even without the region's fish download. World and continent views combine countries' checklists only, so they don't include ocean area additions.
- Stays when you update the catalog or download, update or remove a region.
- Is only on your own checklists. Other people on the same server don't see it.

Species you [import from iNaturalist](#other-taxa) work the same way: importing one adds it to the region you pick, marked **Added by you**.

Adding a species you'd hidden from that region also unhides it there.

To take one off again, open its card's **⋯** menu and click **Remove from this checklist**, or click **×** next to the checklist under **Added by you to:** on the species page. This only removes your addition: if the catalog lists the species there too, it stays. On the species page, an ocean area is marked **(sea)**, and its name opens a nearby region's checklist with just that ocean area ticked.

## Filters, sorting and grouping {#filters}

- **Search this area…** filters the cards by common name, scientific name, other names, or bird code.

**View** sets how the cards are laid out. Under **Arrange**:

- **Group:** **No grouping**, **Broad group** (like Raptors, Owls, Shorebirds, Snakes or Bats), **Family group**, **Rarity**, or (in a region) **Rarity here**.
- **Sort:** **Field guide order** (the default: related species together, as in eBird and printed guides), **Common name**, **Scientific name** (A to Z), **Rarity**, and in a region, **Rarity here** or **Most likely this month**.
- **Size** changes the card size.
- **Photographed first** (on by default), **Seen first** and **Targets first** pin those species to the top.
- **Hide names** for photo-only cards, or to quiz yourself.
- **Show uncollected species in colour** turns off the gray on species you haven't photographed yet. It's off by default, and Lifer remembers your choice.

On a country, state or province, **Counting** holds **Include photos from** (see [Counting only photos from here](#count-photos-from)). How cards name each species and whether they show badges are in [Settings > General](../settings.md#names-and-labels); **Name display settings** at the bottom of **View** goes there.

**Filters** chooses which species show:

- **Animals:** show only some groups, like Birds, Reptiles or Nudibranchs. Tick as many as you like. Only groups you've downloaded for this region are listed, plus a group for each kind of species you've added from iNaturalist (like Insects or Plants).
- **Show:** **All**, **Photographed** (it's in your library), **Seen only** (you saw it but don't have a photo yet), **Targets** (ones you want to find), or **Not yet photographed** (species you haven't seen or photographed).
- **Likely this month:** only species that are regularly recorded in this region in the current month.
- **Photographed in year:** species you photographed in a particular year.
- **Rarely recorded only** and **Not seen in 25 years only** appear when the list has such species (see [badges](#badges)).

A trip's Species view has this same **View** and **Filters**, for the species in that trip (see [Trips](./trips.md#the-trip-page)).

## Ocean areas {#sea-zones}

For fish, a coastal region can include nearby ocean areas: the named seas, gulfs, bays and straits of the International Hydrographic Organization's _Limits of Oceans and Seas_, such as the North Sea, the Gulf of Mexico or the Coral Sea. Where a coast faces open ocean, its ocean area is that country's part of the ocean instead, such as the Portuguese part of the North Atlantic Ocean or the Chilean part of the South Pacific Ocean. Under **Include nearby water:**, tick the ocean areas you want, or **Select all**. Once one is ticked, untick **Include (region)'s own species** to see only the ocean area's fish.

- Ocean areas only add fish, so the control shows when you're viewing all groups or Fish, once the region's fish are downloaded. It also shows whenever you've [added species](#add-to-checklist) to one of the ocean areas yourself.
- If a region has no fish of its own, Lifer ticks its zones for you.
- A country download may bring in the ocean areas its fish checklist needs. You can also download ocean areas from [Regions](./offline-packs-and-map.md).

## Rarity {#rarity-tiers}

Rarity is **how hard a species is to find and photograph** in a place. There are five levels:

| Rarity         | What it means                                                                                              |
| -------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Common**     | An everyday species, seen anywhere, anytime (House Sparrows, Mallards, crows).                             |
| **Occasional** | Findable with a little effort in the right habitat (an Osprey over a lake, a Great Blue Heron in a marsh). |
| **Uncommon**   | Needs a dedicated search. It won't turn up on a casual outing.                                             |
| **Rare**       | Needs travel and patience. Getting a shot is a notable achievement.                                        |
| **Legendary**  | Once in a lifetime: very local or extremely elusive (a Wolverine, a vagrant far from home).                |

**Common** is kept to genuine everyday species, so most species don't pile up at the easy end and each level means something in the field.

### Rarity here and worldwide {#tiers-here-and-worldwide}

A species card can show two rarities:

- **The plain badge** ("Rare") is the **worldwide** rarity: how hard the species is where it's easiest to find, one step harder when its whole range is tiny.
- **The "here" badge** ("Occasional here") is its rarity **in the region you're viewing**. It only appears on a region's checklist. **Rarity here** in Group and Sort uses it.

A bird that's Common worldwide can be Rare here at the edge of its range.

### How rarity is worked out {#how-a-tier-is-worked-out}

- In each province or state, a species is compared with the most-reported species of its group there (the average of the top five). Birds are rated on eBird and GBIF sightings; mammals, reptiles, amphibians and fish on iNaturalist research-grade photos, with an allowance for famous animals people photograph far more often than they find them (a Jaguar). Reported about half as often as the top species or more is Common; the other levels follow at about 1 in 12, 1 in 50 and 1 in 330.
- The same species can differ from place to place. Great Blue Herons are Common in Florida, where they stand on every pond, and Occasional in most other states.
- A **country's** rarity is its easiest well-surveyed province: where you'd go to find it.
- A **vagrant** (records bunched into a year or two, far from its usual range) is Legendary there, with a **Vagrant here** badge. An **introduced** species with an established population is rated like any other, with an **Introduced here** badge.
- Tap a rarity badge to see the numbers behind it. Under **Your rarity** you can set a different rarity if you know better; the card then shows "(yours)".

### What rarity isn't {#what-a-tier-isnt}

- **Not conservation status.** An endangered wader can be on every mudflat, and a species of no concern can be Legendary if it lives in one hard-to-reach spot. The IUCN status is shown beside the rarity.
- **Not a guess.** Where there are too few records or photos to tell, the species shows **Unrated**, or **Not enough data here** on a region's checklist.
- **Marine invertebrates** and species added from iNaturalist aren't rated yet. Their pages show IUCN status instead, where known.

Rarity is refreshed with [catalog updates](../settings.md#species-catalog).

## Badges {#badges}

On species cards:

| Badge                         | Meaning                                                                                           |
| ----------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Endemic**                   | Found in only one country                                                                         |
| **Vagrant**                   | Records here are concentrated in very few years, so it's an irregular visitor                     |
| **Rarely recorded**           | Rarely documented anywhere, but still out there to find                                           |
| **Not seen in 25 years**      | Not recorded anywhere in over 25 years                                                            |
| **Seen again after 25 years** | Was rarely recorded or not seen in 25 years when you photographed it, but isn't any more          |
| **Added by you**              | You added it to this checklist yourself. See [Adding a species to a checklist](#add-to-checklist) |

Species pages can also show **Invasive here**: an established population in this region that's non-native and considered invasive.

## Species almost nobody photographs {#obscure-species}

By default, Lifer hides species almost nobody will encounter from checklists:

- Fish that live deeper than recreational diving depth (60 m). Turn on [Technical diving depths](../settings.md#technical-diving) to use 120 m instead.
- Species with almost no records.
- Species marked **Vagrant** in the region you're viewing.

Turn this off in [Settings > Species and import](../settings.md#obscure-species). Anything you've photographed or seen always stays visible.

## Species pages {#species-page}

Click any card to open its page. It shows:

![A species page](/img/species-detail.jpg)

- Your featured photo, or the reference photo, with arrows to step through reference photos.
- Rarity and badges, a short field-guide paragraph about the species (summarised from Wikipedia) and habitat, and facts that depend on the group, like mass and wingspan for birds, home range for mammals, depth range for fish, and IUCN status.
- A summary of your photos: how many photos, videos, encounters, locations, cameras and lenses, and when you first photographed it.
- **Observations by month** and **by week** charts for the region, and **Where to find it**, a map of hotspots (needs the [offline map](./offline-packs-and-map.md#offline-map)). For widespread species it says so instead, with a link to recent sightings on iNaturalist.
- Links to the species on iNaturalist and eBird (birds only).
- **Your photos**, with a **View** menu for **Sort** (**Newest first**, **Oldest first**, **Highest rated first**), **Size**, and **Labels** (here the date and place), **Camera info** and **Ratings** (with the **Best shot** badge), the same choices as every other page with photos, filters for **All**, **Edited**, **RAW** and **Video**, and **Upload** to add more. Opened from a [trip](./trips.md#the-trip-page), the photos from that trip come first under their own heading, then **Other photos**. Click a heading to fold it away.

Next to the name, **Seen** and **Target** turn those on and off (each shows a checkmark while it's on, and the message after it has **Undo**). A species you've photographed shows **Photographed** instead of **Seen**. The **⋯** menu beside them has the less common actions: **Archive species**, **Add to another checklist** (see [Adding a species to a checklist](#add-to-checklist)), and **Adjust card preview** (the crop used on the species card, once you have a featured photo).

![The About section and your photos on a species page](/img/species-about.jpg)

Each photo's **⋯** menu has **Rate**, **Set as featured photo** (or **Remove as featured photo**, which goes back to Lifer's own pick), **Add to album**, **Download original**, **Download RAW**, **Also features another species**, **Correct the ID**, **Location** (with the photo's place under it), **Edit tags**, **Hide photo** and **Delete photo**. Each of these that changes the photo opens its own dialog. **Location** opens the same dialog as the map: pick a saved place, type or paste coordinates, or pick on the map, and **Edit name and region** changes the place name and region on their own. A message offers **Undo** after you save. **Correct the ID** lists the photo's likeliest other species above the search. After you pick one, a message offers **View** to go to the new species and **Undo** to move the photo back. **Hide photo** keeps it out of your life list without deleting it; find it under **Hidden** in the Gallery's filters (see [Hidden photos](./culling-with-other-apps.md#hidden)). On the desktop app it also has **Show in Finder** and **Copy file path**, one for each file when a photo has a RAW and an edited file.

![A photo's menu on a species page](/img/photo-menu.jpg)

To change many photos at once, click **Select**, then click photos (<kbd>Shift</kbd>-click selects a range). The toolbar has **Correct ID…**, **Add tag…**, **Rate…**, **Hide** and **Delete selected**.

## Insects, plants and other groups {#other-taxa}

For groups Lifer has no checklists for, like insects, spiders, plants and fungi, you can add species from iNaturalist, one at a time or a whole list at once.

1. Turn on [Search iNaturalist for other species](../settings.md#any-taxa-search) in **Settings > Species and import**.
2. In **Add a species from iNaturalist**, click **Search**. (With the setting on, quick search also offers **Search iNaturalist** when you type a species Lifer doesn't have.)
3. Choose **One species** to search and add a single species, or **Import a list** to paste many names at once, then pick a country, or a province or state.

See [iNaturalist](./inaturalist.md#any-taxa) for the details.

These species appear on that region's checklist marked **Added by you** (and on the checklists of the regions that contain it, like the country for a province). They're yours: other people on the same server don't see them unless they add them too. To put one on another region's checklist too, open its species page and click **Add to another checklist** (see [Adding a species to a checklist](#add-to-checklist)). Filter to them with their group in **Animals**, like **Insects**. They have a photo and description from iNaturalist, but no rarity or occurrence data.
